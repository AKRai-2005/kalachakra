The question nobody can answer today

A classifier scores the traffic it observed. It cannot represent an action that was never taken, so it cannot answer the question a defender actually asks: what happens if I isolate this host now? KALACHAKRA learns the transition dynamics of a host-interaction graph and rolls them forward six steps with no new observations, which makes that question expressible.

Our headline claim is refuted, by our own experiments

We predicted a world model would warn earlier than a classifier. It does not. On CTU-13 - thirteen real botnet captures, 19.9 million flows, leave-one-scenario-out - it catches 5 of 12 infections at a 10% false-alarm budget. A plain gradient-boosted tree catches 7 to 9, and logistic regression still wins AUC and F1.

We published 1 of 12. It was wrong, and finding out is the contribution

Our own published figure made us look worse than we were, and we corrected it anyway. Experiment 16 could not reproduce it; a deterministic control (gradient boosting, which reproduced the old row to the digit) proved the pipeline was identical; six seeds ruled out noise; and experiment 18 found the cause.

Detections and AUC move in opposite directions: epochs against detections r = -0.451, epochs against AUC r = +0.780. Our earlier tuning pass selected on AUC, so it was steering away from the metric we report, and its grid began at 25 epochs - outside the region that works (12 to 18). A selection criterion that is not the reported metric needs an argument that the two agree. Ours did not.

Why this is worth a judge's time

Every experiment's predictions were written into its file before it ran, and the run prints the verdict; 16 of 40 failed and all 16 are published. The instrument that refuted our model is reusable by anyone working this corpus, and it caught an error in our own published number. A result that only ever confirms its authors is the one to distrust.

The console replays a finished run - we say so rather than implying a live service. Every number here comes from a verdict file in the repository.
